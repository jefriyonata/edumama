Finding the right preschool or kindergarten is one of the first big decisions expat families make after arriving in Jakarta. The city has an impressive range of English-language and international early-years options — from prestigious through-schools that run from kindergarten to Grade 12, to warm, dedicated preschools focused purely on the early years.

This guide explains how early-years education works in Jakarta for expat parents — the local terminology, the curricula you'll encounter, the best areas for international families, and **10 of the top preschools and kindergartens** with indicative fees so you can shortlist quickly. If you're still arranging day-to-day care, start with our companion [guide to daycare in Jakarta](/en/articles/daycare-jakarta-expat-guide).

## Preschool, Kindergarten, Playgroup: The Local Terms

Indonesia's early-years vocabulary trips up a lot of newcomers, because the labels don't map neatly onto the English ones. In practice, "preschool" and "kindergarten" are used interchangeably here, and most international schools cover both under one early-years division.

* **PAUD** (*Pendidikan Anak Usia Dini*) — the umbrella term for all early-childhood education, roughly ages 0–6.
* **Kelompok Bermain (KB)** — "playgroup," usually around 18 months to 3 years.
* **Taman Kanak-Kanak (TK)** — kindergarten, usually ages 4–6, often split into TK A and TK B.
* **Preschool / nursery** — the English terms international schools use, generally covering ages ~2–5.

<Callout type="info">
  When you enquire locally, the words **PAUD, KB, and TK** help you find the right programme and age group. International schools will simply talk about "early years," "preschool," "nursery," or "kindergarten."
</Callout>

## Types of Early-Years School in Jakarta

Expat families generally choose from four broad categories. The right one depends on the language environment you want, your budget, and how long you plan to stay.

| Type | Language | Curriculum | Typical cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| International school (early years) | English (or bilingual) | Foreign (British, IB, Australian…) | Rp 100–200 million+/year |
| Montessori preschool | Bilingual / English | Montessori | Rp 30–90 million/year |
| Bilingual / "national plus" | English + Indonesian | National + enrichment | Rp 25–80 million/year |
| Local TK / PAUD | Indonesian | National (PAUD) | Rp 0.5–2 million/month |

### International schools

These run in English (or English-first bilingual), follow a foreign curriculum, and their early-years divisions often sit within a larger K–12 school. They're the priciest option but give your child a fully English-speaking early-years experience — the usual choice for families on shorter postings.

### Montessori preschools

Jakarta has a healthy Montessori scene, from small independent houses to established schools. Montessori is a child-led approach built around hands-on, self-correcting materials and mixed-age classes; the preschools that follow it are often bilingual and sit in the mid-to-upper price range.

### Bilingual and "national plus" preschools

A popular middle ground: an Indonesian foundation with significant English and added enrichment. More affordable than international schools, with a mixed-language environment many families like.

### Local TK / PAUD

Neighbourhood kindergartens are the most affordable and the most immersive way for a child to pick up Bahasa Indonesia — taught almost entirely in Indonesian.

## Curricula You'll Come Across

Early-years schools in Jakarta advertise a range of approaches. The common ones:

* **British EYFS** — the Early Years Foundation Stage, play-based and structured; feeds into British-curriculum schools.
* **IB PYP (early years)** — inquiry-led, part of the International Baccalaureate continuum.
* **Montessori** — child-led, hands-on, mixed-age classes with self-correcting materials.
* **Reggio Emilia–inspired** — project- and creativity-focused, child-directed.
* **Singaporean** — structured, strong on early numeracy and literacy.
* **Play-based / eclectic** — many bilingual schools blend elements of the above.

<Callout type="tip">
  Don't over-index on the label. A warm, well-run play-based school will serve a two- or three-year-old better than a prestigious curriculum delivered without care. Visit, watch how staff interact with the children, and trust what you see.
</Callout>

### 1. Jakarta Intercultural School (JIS)

The oldest and most prestigious international school in the city, **JIS** runs an inquiry-based international programme (IB continuum) across campuses in Pondok Indah, Cilandak, and Pantai Indah Kapuk. Its Early Childhood division starts from age 3. Expect the highest fees in Jakarta: early-childhood tuition starts around **Rp 130 million/year**, but mandatory annual capital fees and one-off charges push the real first-year cost well above Rp 200 million. Best for families wanting a blue-chip, globally recognised school with deep resources.

### 2. British School Jakarta (BSJ)

Set on a large green campus in **Bintaro**, **BSJ** follows the British **Early Years Foundation Stage (EYFS)** and welcomes families from more than 50 nationalities. It takes children from age 2 (Nursery) through to Year 13. Early-years tuition starts around **Rp 150 million/year** and rises through the kindergarten years, with a one-time application fee and refundable enrolment deposit on top. A strong choice for British-curriculum families.

### 3. Australian Independent School (AIS) Jakarta

**AIS** offers the Australian curriculum alongside the IB, with early-learning campuses in **Pejaten and Kemang** — right in the heart of expat South Jakarta. It accepts children from around 2–3 years. Early-years costs sit at roughly **Rp 100–165 million/year** (the first year is higher because of one-off fees). A good middle option for an established international school without the very top-tier price tag.

### 4. Nord Anglia School Jakarta (NAS)

Part of the global Nord Anglia family, **NAS Jakarta** (in **Cilandak**) follows the English National Curriculum and takes its youngest learners from **18 months** in a purpose-built early-years area with soft-play and messy-play zones. First-year enrolment for a two-year-old is around **Rp 120 million**, plus a non-refundable application fee. Appealing for families who value the Nord Anglia global network and early starting age.

### 5. New Zealand School Jakarta (NZSJ)

Tucked into leafy **Kemang**, **NZSJ** follows New Zealand's **Te Whāriki** early-years framework — a warm, play-based approach centred on the whole child — and accepts children from 18 months. It's one of the more accessible international options at this age: a half-day pre-school place starts around **Rp 58 million/year**. Great for families who want a nurturing, play-first environment.

### 6. North Jakarta Intercultural School (NJIS)

For families in **North Jakarta**, **NJIS** in **Kelapa Gading** runs a hands-on preschool programme for ages 2–4 with small classes (around 10–15 students) and internationally qualified teachers. Fees are provided on request. A convenient, well-regarded international option away from the South Jakarta cluster.

### 7. Springfield School

**Springfield** operates modern campuses in **Permata Buana (West Jakarta)** and **Cibubur (East Jakarta)**, making it handy for families outside the usual south-side hubs. It's an international through-school for ages 2 to 18, with an early-years programme that blends US, UK, and Singaporean resources. Fees are on request. Worth a look if you're based in the west or east of the city.

### 8. Kinderland

A long-running **Singapore-based** preschool brand, **Kinderland** has several Jakarta campuses (including Lebak Bulus and Muara Karang) and takes children from 18 months to 6 years. Fees run roughly **Rp 40–80 million/year** — a big step down from the full international schools — making it a popular, more affordable bilingual-friendly choice.

### 9. Global Sevilla

**Global Sevilla** runs preschool and kindergarten at its **Puri Indah (West)** and **Pulo Mas (East)** campuses, using the **EYFS** curriculum with added mindfulness activities. It's among the most budget-friendly on this list: toddler tuition from around **Rp 25 million/year** rising to about **Rp 45 million/year** for kindergarten, plus a one-off development fee. A solid value pick for west/east Jakarta families.

### 10. Tutor Time

An established international preschool and kindergarten brand operating since 1996, **Tutor Time** has its flagship in **Pondok Indah** plus branches in North and West Jakarta, serving ages 1–6 in an English-language, play-based environment. Fees vary by centre and by how many sessions a week your child attends, and sit well below full international-school tuition — confirm the current schedule with your nearest campus.

## How Much Do Preschools & Kindergartens Cost?

Fees vary more than almost anything else about Jakarta schooling. As a rough 2026 guide:

| Tier | What you get | Typical cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local TK / PAUD | Indonesian-language, simple facilities | Rp 0.5–2 million/month |
| Bilingual / value preschool | English + Indonesian, enrichment | Rp 25–80 million/year |
| Montessori | Bilingual, Montessori materials | Rp 30–90 million/year |
| International school (early years) | English, foreign curriculum | Rp 100–200 million/year+ |

International schools usually charge **per term or per year** and add **one-time fees** — registration, enrolment, and sometimes a refundable deposit or development levy — that can run into the tens of millions of rupiah on top of tuition. Always ask for the full fee schedule, not just the headline tuition.

## Best Areas for Expat Families

Most international preschools and kindergartens cluster in **South Jakarta (Jakarta Selatan)** and a few family-friendly satellite townships:

* **Kemang** — the heart of expat Jakarta; several international preschools and playgroups.
* **Cipete & Pejaten** — leafy and quieter, popular with young families.
* **Pondok Indah** — upscale, home to major international schools and their early-years programmes.
* **Kebayoran Baru & Senopati** — central and convenient for working parents.
* **BSD City & Bintaro** — spacious southern townships with several international schools; a common pick for longer-term families with cars.

Locally, searches often split by zone — *preschool Jakarta Selatan, Barat, Timur, Pusat* — so narrow to your neighbourhood when you shortlist.

## How to Choose a Preschool or Kindergarten

A few questions help narrow the field quickly:

1. **Location and commute** — Jakarta traffic is real; a school close to home matters enormously for a young child.
2. **Curriculum and continuity** — if you may relocate again, a widely recognised curriculum (British, IB, Australian) travels well.
3. **Accreditation & licensing** — a registered school, and for international schools, recognised accreditation (e.g. CIS).
4. **Teacher qualifications** — early-years training and, for English settings, genuinely fluent staff.
5. **Class size and ratios** — smaller is better for this age.
6. **Total cost** — look past headline tuition to registration, capital/development fees, deposits, and extras.
7. **Language environment** — fully English, bilingual, or Indonesian?
8. **A visit and trial** — see a normal session and, ideally, let your child try a taster day.

## Language and Cultural Notes

* **Language:** International schools run in English; bilingual and local schools weave in — or lead with — Bahasa Indonesia, which can be a lovely head start on the local language.
* **Food:** Snacks and meals are usually rice-based and halal; flag allergies and dietary needs in writing.
* **Holidays:** Expect closures around **Idul Fitri (Lebaran)** and other national and religious holidays, plus each school's own term breaks.
* **Academic year:** International schools frequently run an **August–June** year, while national schools run **July–June** — worth checking if you're timing a move.

## Practical Tips for Expat Parents

* **Enquire early.** The most popular international schools keep **waitlists** — reach out before you arrive if you can.
* **Have documents ready.** Passport, immunisation records, previous school reports, and your family's KITAS/visa details are commonly requested.
* **Visit in person.** Photos and prospectuses only tell you so much; a short visit tells you far more.
* **Tap the community.** Expat parent groups and neighbourhood chats are the best source of honest, current feedback.
